**What this shows**
- **On a verifier-scored repair test, the infused skill's TRM-side pieces beat the Jev in both roles.**
  - Repairing: dual_repair fixes four times as many candidates as a Jev writing the grid.
  - Deciding: a 9.7k-parameter recursive policy trained on 3,009 synthetic items is at the menu
    ceiling. It needs no post-repair check to know which cases are unfixable.
- **The Jev is no better than the generic Qwen here** (D2). It was never trained on Campsite grids.
  The loop helps it as a decider (0.643 vs 0.569), mainly by rejecting fewer fixable items.
- **The skill's own flow policies never reject.**
  - dual_repair_if_any_sig_fail matches the TRM's success (0.731) but commits a broken grid on 27%
    of items: every swap_rect and bad_shape item, plus 9 move_in_row items.
  - The gap between these policies and the Decision-TRM is the reject decision, not the repair.
- **The CSP solver solves every puzzle.** On puzzles this small, discarding the candidate and
  re-solving dominates any repair.

**Limits, from the spec.**
- The candidates are synthetic perturbations, the puzzles are at most 6x6, and the deciders do not
  see post-repair verification.
- The Decision-TRM was trained on the same perturbation types it is tested on (new puzzles, same
  defect mix). The Jev saw four examples.
- So D1 compares a model trained in-distribution with a few-shot one.
